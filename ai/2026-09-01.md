## Counter-narrative

Skeptical case on the day's biggest story (Apple CEO transition):

- **"AI becomes Job No. 1" is a headline framing, not necessarily a directional signal.** Every incoming Apple CEO in the modern era has inherited a strategic-uncertainty narrative on the highest-profile technology of the day — Cook inherited "post-Jobs product coherence," Ternus inherits "generative AI catch-up." The narrative predicts less about actual direction than about the pressure the CEO is under to be seen doing something. A defensible reading is that Ternus's first six months will look like continuity — same partner discussions, same on-device/PCC split, same feature staggering by region — precisely because reversing course early would concede that the Cook-era posture was wrong. Watch the second-half signals, not the first.
- **A hardware-lead CEO is not axiomatically weaker on AI.** The dominant frame today is that Ternus's hardware background is a liability for a software-and-services problem, but Apple's actual AI moat, to the extent it has one, runs through silicon (Neural Engine, unified memory, on-device inference power envelope) and the Ternus organisation has owned that stack for years. A less obvious reading is that Ternus is the CEO best positioned to align chip roadmap with on-device model roadmap in a way that his predecessor could not, and that the visible "software direction" story is downstream of that alignment.

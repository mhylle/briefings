## Headlines

> **Anthropic: Claude formalises Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean in 11 days on an internal Fable-5.1-tier model** — *Anthropic / arXiv companion PDF, published 2026-09-04, wider coverage cycle 09-04 → 09-05*
> Anthropic published the write-up and code (github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem) for the first end-to-end, computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean. Multi-agent workflow: dozens of Claude Code agents operating in parallel against a directed-acyclic-graph of theorem statements maintained by **Prove2Me**, an open platform built by Tianyi Peng's Columbia University group (Peng is now an Anthropic researcher). 13M lines of Lean, 30,300 theorems proved (29,500 used in the final proof — over 5× the size of Mathlib), ~6B output tokens on an internal general-purpose research model Anthropic describes as roughly comparable to Fable 5.1. Uses only Lean's three standard axioms, no omitted proofs, follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition of Wiles's 1995 argument. Kevin Buzzard (Imperial College, lead of the community FLT-in-Lean project) endorses on the record: "extraordinary autoformalization achievement… we have taken a big step towards automatic formalization of the modern mathematical literature." Anthropic's first attempt (on Wiles direct) failed; Prove2Me + multi-agent orchestration was the mechanism that worked. Closes the last entry on Freek Wiedijk's 100-formalisation-challenge list, a 20-year benchmark. **Why it matters:** three separable enterprise reads. (1) *Capability signal.* This is the first commercially-adjacent, third-party-verified demonstration that agentic Lean work at Mathlib-scale is now a fortnight of agent time on an internal, Fable-5.1-adjacent model, not years of expert human work. For any enterprise workflow with a formal-verification tail — cryptographic protocol correctness, seL4-style microkernel guarantees, safety-critical control code, financial-model soundness proofs — the cost curve of formal verification has just shifted materially. Deliverable within the next quarter: assess where in the internal SDLC formal-verification cost previously ruled itself out on effort grounds, and re-scope. (2) *Autoformalisation, not autonomous discovery.* Claude reformalised a known human proof (Wiles), building on Mathlib and Buzzard's own project — Buzzard is explicit that the achievement is autoformalisation of the modern literature, not novel mathematical creation. Enterprise procurement teams sold on "AI now does mathematics" as a category should treat this as evidence for automated proof-engineering, not for autonomous discovery. (3) *Mechanism generalises beyond mathematics.* Prove2Me's DAG-of-goals-plus-multi-agent-workflow-plus-Claude-Code pattern is directly analogous to the architecture pattern that enterprise agent frameworks are converging on for long-horizon software work; the mathematical demonstration is the visible manifestation of a general workflow shape that shipped commercially last week (Cursor's Fable-5.1 integration with self-verification of code as it writes). Expect follow-on demonstrations from Google DeepMind (AlphaProof lineage) inside 4–8 weeks. **EU/Nordic impact:** the AI Act's Article 55 obligations on GPAI systemic-risk models include documentation of capabilities and limitations; a demonstration of this magnitude, on a "roughly Fable-5.1" internal model, is a data point that should factor into any GPAI classification and risk-tier assessment inside the EU AI-Office's autumn Article-11 audit wave. ## Counter-narrative

Skeptical case on the day's biggest story (Claude's Fermat formalisation):

- **This is autoformalisation, not autonomous discovery.** Buzzard's own framing — "autoformalization of the modern mathematical literature" — is precise, and it is what Anthropic actually did. Claude reformalised Wiles's 1995 argument via the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition, standing on top of Mathlib and Buzzard's Imperial College FLT project. Framings in secondary coverage that lean on "AI proved Fermat's Last Theorem" collapse the distinction between (a) rendering a known human proof machine-checkable and (b) discovering novel mathematics; only (a) is demonstrated. Enterprise readers should discount marketing language on this correspondingly.
- **The "11 days, largely autonomous" framing understates the human infrastructure.** The Prove2Me DAG-of-goals platform is the reason multi-agent orchestration worked; that platform was built by a Columbia group over the preceding period, is now a formal Anthropic investment (Peng is an Anthropic researcher), and is not itself a Claude output. Anthropic's own initial attempt (on Wiles direct, without Prove2Me) failed. The correct summary is "the right agentic-orchestration platform, plus dozens of agents on a Fable-5.1-tier model, running 11 days, wrote 13M lines of Lean" — impressive, but the platform is a substantial pre-existing human artefact. For enterprises inferring generalisation to their own workloads, the base rate to plan against is "agentic orchestration + task-specific platform + significant sustained agent compute," not "point a model at the problem."
- **Model attribution is deliberately ambiguous.** "An internal general-purpose research model roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1" is neither Fable 5.1 nor a disclosed variant; the exact capability provenance of the run is not third-party-verifiable. For enterprise capability planning, treat the demonstration as evidence about what "roughly Fable-5.1-tier + Prove2Me + multi-agent Claude Code" can do, not as evidence about Fable 5.1 the commercial product specifically.

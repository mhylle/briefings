> **California signs AB 1405 (state AI Auditor Registry) and SB 813 (independent-audit requirement) — first US state requiring independent AI audits, effective 1 Jan 2029** — *State of California, 2026-09-09 late US / in-window UTC*
> Governor Gavin Newsom signed **AB 1405** (Bauer-Kahan, D-Orinda) and **SB 813** into law on the evening of 09-Sept. AB 1405 creates a **state-run AI Auditor Registry** at the Government Operations Agency, with registration and misconduct-reporting mechanisms live no later than **1 January 2029**; from that date, **no one without a state registration number may conduct a "covered AI audit."** SB 813 defines the "covered AI audit" — an assessment of internal controls, processes or systems needed for compliance with state AI-related laws. Together the two bills establish the first US state-level analogue to the EU AI Act's independent-third-party-audit posture. Newsom framed the pair as "first-in-the-nation AI safeguards to protect Californians" and called on the federal government to match. **Why it matters:** three reads. (1) *This is the first US state to legislate the "AI companies cannot be their own auditors" principle at the statutory level, and California-first-mover always sets a template.* Sixteen other US states have AI-audit or AI-transparency bills at various procedural stages (NY SB 5641 the closest analogue; Colorado AI Act already partly in force); expect at least 4–6 to follow California's shape inside 12 months. - **Oracle's $664B RPO is time-weighted, not near-term revenue.** RPO is defined as contracted-but-not-yet-recognised revenue over the *entire life* of contracts; Oracle disclosed on the call that a large fraction of the $664B is on 5-to-10-year AI-cloud commitments (Stargate/OpenAI-linked contracts prominent among them).

## Headlines

> **Microsoft AI publishes Code of Conduct for MAI Models — six-week public consultation opens** — *Microsoft AI, 14-Sept 2026 (in-window)*
> Microsoft AI opened a public consultation on its Code of Conduct for MAI Models, a 37-page (~15,000-word) governance document covering MAI-Thinking-1, MAI-Code-1.1-Flash, MAI-Image-2.6, MAI-Transcribe-2 and MAI-Voice-2. The framing is "Humanist AI": models are "subordinate, aligned, and contained," people matter more than AI, and AI must never resist being switched off. Four Absolute Constraints bar MAI models from (a) assisting with chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear or explosive weapons; (b) providing operational capability for offensive cyberattacks (defensive-only work permitted); (c) evading or defeating human oversight; and (d) manipulating people harmfully at scale, including through systematic disinformation or coordinated influence operations. Consultation runs six weeks with a revised version planned later in 2026. Why it matters: this is the first hyperscaler-authored humanist-AI text and gives EU regulators (Article 56 Code-of-Practice signatories) and enterprise procurement a concrete first-party document to reference — but note it is a *self-authored governance instrument* Microsoft controls, materially different in operational-posture kind from Anthropic's persistent-employee-like external-evaluator commitment. The "no resisting shutdown" clause explicitly maps to one of the six risk domains in the September 2025 Global Call for AI Red Lines (loss of control), which pairs the document with the UNGA red-lines agenda now nine days out. **EU/Nordic impact:** direct — the document is a candidate reference for the EU AI Office's Codes-of-Practice interpretation and for Nordic public-sector AI procurement standards on GPAI systemic-risk models. ## Themes & signals

**The Pace-the-Frontier arc has now hit political ceilings in both major powers on the same day.** The 24-hour window that produced Microsoft's Code of Conduct and Amodei's Anthropic-embedded-evaluator commitment also produced (a) VP-level regulatory-capture framing in the US and (b) MFA-level anti-containment framing in China. This is a materially different equilibrium than one week ago, when the pacing-consensus vector looked like it might carry into statutory outcomes: the operational-artifact vector (Microsoft's Code of Conduct, Anthropic's embedded evaluators, Google DeepMind's Singapore double-blind pilot, xAI's silence, Salesforce/Anthropic Claudeforce) continues to compound, while the *statutory* vector has been systematically ceilinged from both directions. Read this as the "pacing without statute" scenario hardening into the base case for at least the next 12 months.

**The four operational shapes of the pacing pledge are now legible enough to price into procurement.** (i) Anthropic — embedded external evaluators with employee-like access and publish-without-edit rights; (ii) Microsoft — self-authored governance artifact under six-week public consultation with four Absolute Constraints; (iii) Google DeepMind — cryptographic double-blind pilot (Singapore) with no governance-artifact commitment yet; (iv) xAI — silence, no operational commitment on record. These are not interchangeable, and vendor-diligence must not treat them as equivalent because they landed inside the same 96-hour window. Microsoft's Code of Conduct is a valuable EU-facing document but does *not* substitute for external oversight; Anthropic's embedded-evaluator commitment is the strongest single operational posture on offer; DeepMind's cryptographic evaluation is genuinely differentiated but non-persistent; xAI's silence prices as opt-out until proven otherwise.

**Chinese-market capital signals continue to sit orthogonal to the Western pacing debate.** Z.ai's $5B follow-on today, DeepSeek's ~$75B pre-IPO Shanghai STAR trajectory, and Zhipu's HKEX filing pattern all say the same thing: PRC public and quasi-public capital is willing to fund frontier-lab capex at scale and quickly, and it does so without any equivalent to the Anthropic-Microsoft governance-artifact bilateral. Beijing's MOFA and MOFCOM statements today make explicit what Chinese capital markets have been implicit about all quarter: no participation in the Anthropic-led safety framing, no acceptance of the CISA/FBI/NSA distillation attribution, no international-consensus posture. The "pacing consensus" is a Western-frontier-lab-and-hyperscaler consensus, not a global one.

**Notably absent today:** any formal statement from Google DeepMind matching Microsoft's governance-artifact publication (rumored inside 2–4 weeks — clock now running for one calendar week); any xAI operational-posture statement; any Alibaba corporate response to Anthropic's distillation attribution (only the MOFA + MOFCOM government statements landed); any OpenAI operational detail on its Sept-12 embedded-evaluator commitment; any EU AI Office statement referencing today's Microsoft Code of Conduct. ## Counter-narrative

Skeptical case on the day's biggest story (Microsoft MAI Code of Conduct publication + Vance/Trump/MOFA political counter-triangle):

- **The Microsoft Code of Conduct's Absolute Constraints are exactly the shape of policy Microsoft would write to *pre-empt* stricter external oversight.** The four constraints (CBRN, offensive-cyber, evading-oversight, mass-manipulation) map cleanly onto the six risk domains in the Sept-2025 Global Call for AI Red Lines — but they map to the domains *hyperscalers already publicly agree with* (CBRN and offensive-cyber are consensus red lines even from lab-side commentators), and they *do not* commit Microsoft to persistent external-evaluator access, publish-without-edit rights, or third-party operational scrutiny. A well-informed skeptic would say: this is a self-authored document Microsoft can revise on its own schedule (six-week consultation, then Microsoft-decides-what-changed), and it consumes the *governance-artifact slot* the EU AI Office might otherwise have used to demand something structurally similar to Anthropic's embedded-evaluator commitment. Read the document not as the ceiling on Microsoft governance but as the *floor of what Microsoft would have had to accept externally* — and note the gap.
- **The Vance/Trump/MOFA counter-triangle may not be the political ceiling it looks like — it may be the acceleration signal.** A skeptic would argue that VP-level regulatory-capture framing and MFA-level anti-containment framing are *precisely* the political conditions in which frontier labs push governance artifacts they control rather than accept externally imposed ones. Under this reading, Microsoft's Code of Conduct is the first-mover in a genre — self-authored humanist-AI text that occupies the "we are the responsible party" slot before any government can define it — and Vance's "Trojan horse" framing accidentally *helps* the operational-posture-artifact strategy by ensuring nothing statutory competes with it in the US market for 3–5 years. The pacing-consensus vector isn't losing to the political counter-vector; the two vectors are running on different tracks, and today is the day both compounded.

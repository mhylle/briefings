## Counter-narrative

Skeptical case on the day's biggest story (**Anthropic Claude Sonnet 5.5's Terminal-Bench 4.0 leap from 10.3% to 70.6% at unchanged pricing**):

- **The 6.9× benchmark jump is partly benchmark-generation gain, not model gain.** Terminal-Bench 4.0 is a new revision — Sonnet 5's 10.3% baseline is not on the *same* Terminal-Bench version Sonnet 5.5 scores 70.6% on unless Anthropic explicitly retested Sonnet 5 on Terminal-Bench 4.0. A skeptic would read the model card carefully for the retest disclosure; if Sonnet 5's score is from Terminal-Bench 3.x, the delta is contaminated. Anthropic's blog post does frame it as Sonnet 5.5 vs Sonnet 5 on the same eval, but the arXiv methodology note has not accompanied the release, so external replication is not yet possible.
- **Same pricing is not the same total cost of ownership.** Anthropic quotes "up to 30% less per task" via latency + reduced token usage; a skeptic notes that "up to" language is the marketing-adjective substitute for a distribution. If your workload is at the low end of that distribution, TCO may be unchanged; if at the high end, the savings materialize. Real workload benchmarks are needed before the pricing anchor delivers.
- **Sonnet-5-anchored pricing may not survive Haiku 5.5.** Anthropic has a track record of introducing a new-tier low-cost anchor with each Haiku release; Haiku 5.5 could reset the pricing envelope in a way that makes Sonnet 5.5's "unchanged" pricing anchor less durable than it looks today. The Sonnet-5.5-at-Sonnet-5-pricing move is best read as *temporary anchor discipline* pending Haiku 5.5, not as a permanent statement of Sonnet-tier pricing.
- **Positioned inside a pace-the-frontier posture, the Sonnet 5.5 ship is capability-cheap for Anthropic to compensate for the IPO-delay optics.** A skeptic could argue Sonnet 5.5's release timing (28-Sept, the day before DevDay, six days after Opus 5.5, the same day as OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Astra scrap disclosure) is *tactically* aligned to give Anthropic a shipping-cadence narrative advantage while its IPO calendar slips past midterms. The tactical reading is not incompatible with the durable-capability reading, but enterprise architects should hold both.
- **Alternative counter-narrative on Nvidia OASP:** the 100+ partner-count is the marketing headline; a skeptic would ask how many of those partners have *committed to production deployment* vs. how many are on the launch-partner press release. Cisco / Microsoft / Oracle / CoreWeave are strong signals; the tail 90 partners have not been named individually in first-day coverage. Also: the *"quarantine in milliseconds"* claim requires Vera CPUs — this is not a software-only solution and locks the tooling substrate to Nvidia's next-gen hardware roadmap. The EU sovereignty implication (non-EU-designed CPU as the substrate for EU AI-agent safety) will be picked up by at least one European regulator inside a quarter.

## Counter-narrative

Skeptical case on the day's biggest story (**Marvell's $94B 2028 data-center TAM + 18 current custom-silicon sockets**):

- **Investor-Day TAM figures are vendor-authored.** $94B is Marvell's own 2028 TAM aggregate; buy-side analysts have historically applied 30-50% discounts to vendor TAM claims. A rigorous skeptic reads $94B as the top of a range whose base case may be materially lower, especially given multi-year hyperscaler capex cycles that can defer or re-scope commitments.
- **18 current sockets vs >50 pipeline is a disclosure asymmetry.** The 18 are contracted; the >50 are "pipeline opportunities" — which spans RFP, LOI, prototyping, and committed-but-pre-contract stages. Treating $75B as "pipeline lifetime revenue" assumes conversion rates that custom-silicon ASIC deals historically do not hit in aggregate.
- **The 2H FY27 ramp timing is a slip from earlier framing.** Marvell's prior investor framing had custom-silicon acceleration more front-weighted; the shift to 2H FY27 is a 2-quarter deferral. A skeptic reads the Investor-Day TAM-lift as partially compensatory optics around a schedule push.
- **The named-hyperscaler wins (AMZN/MSFT/GOOGL) overlap with existing public roadmaps** (Trainium, Maia, Axion/TPU adjacencies). The specific economic exposure to Marvell within each hyperscaler's custom-silicon stack is not disclosed, so socket count ≠ revenue share.
- **The $94B TAM anchors the thesis but does not clarify Marvell's capturable share.** A $94B TAM with 10% share is a materially different investment than a $94B TAM with 30% share, and Marvell did not re-anchor its share assumption at this event.

## Counter-narrative

Skeptical case on today's biggest story (**the two-day governance-substrate pause as operative signal**):

- **A two-day pause is not a pattern.** Weekends routinely produce 2-3 day quiet stretches in both US regulatory motion and vendor PR calendars. 2026-10-07 was a Wednesday and 2026-10-08 is a Thursday — midweek — so weekend-pattern is a weak counter, but holidays, agency-specific calendars, and simple lag between service-of-process and public filing can account for the pause without any substrate-exhaustion interpretation. Reading two days as a cadence-break risks overfitting on a short window.
- **The absence of a public filing is not the absence of activity.** CA AG, FTC and Senate References are each on their own statutory clocks; motion inside those clocks (deposition requests, document production, inter-agency coordination) is not required to produce public artifacts in any given 24-hour window. Treating "no public artifact" as "no motion" is a visibility bias.
- **Vendor silence on Marvell's $94B TAM may reflect banker-prep rather than acquiescence.** Nvidia and AMD both have Q3 earnings inside ~3 weeks. A pre-earnings public response to a competitor TAM claim is standard-not-done. Marvell's narrative is unopposed in this window because every incumbent is in their own quiet period, not because none has an answer.
- **The Haiku 5.5 "Day 16 overdue" framing assumes a tight reading of "coming weeks."** Three weeks is still inside "weeks" plural; four weeks remains defensible. The anchor-pressure interpretation is sound but the hardening framing should not harden faster than the actual calendar warrants.
- **"Nobody is first-mover on counter-anchor" is also "the market agrees with the anchor"** — which is not an alarm signal for buyers of custom-silicon capacity, it's a stability signal.

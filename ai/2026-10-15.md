## Counter-narrative

**ASML Q3 2026 beat + raised is the dominant in-window story.** The skeptical case:

- **The Q4 €14.2-15.2B guide is a ~26% sequential step at the midpoint, which is unusually large.** The honest read is that it depends on precise shipment timing of a small number of High-NA + EUV tools; one or two tools slipping into Q1 2027 would compress Q4 by €0.5-1.0B and expand Q1 commensurately — the demand signal would be unchanged but the headline Q4 figure would miss guide. Analysts anchoring 2027 compute-capex-plan forecasts to ASML's Q4 should haircut for shipment-timing risk, not just demand risk. The FY range anchoring to the lower half of Q4 guide (€44-45B FY implies Q4 of ~€13.9-14.5B at midpoint, i.e., slightly below Q4 guide midpoint) is management already pricing in some timing-slip risk — follow their lead rather than reading the Q4 guide midpoint as deterministic.
- **The "AI demand" causal attribution is doing a lot of work.** Memory-related net system sales were guided at +75% in July; the Q3 beat reflects timing + mix as much as end-demand. The direct read-through from ASML equipment shipments to GPU/accelerator supply availability is on 18-24 month leads with multiple intermediate chokepoints (TSMC/Samsung/Intel fab utilization; HBM supply; packaging/CoWoS capacity); treating the ASML print as a direct Q4-2026-and-Q1-2027 GPU-supply signal overstates the causal link.
- **China exposure holds at ~20% of 2026 sales.** The Oct 1 report of some advanced-computing export controls being rescinded was not amended in ASML's Q3 release; the regulatory-risk tail on 2027 China revenue is unchanged. If BIS or Commerce reverses direction in the next 60 days, that is a material downside not priced into current guidance.

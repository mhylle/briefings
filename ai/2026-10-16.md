## Counter-narrative

**On today's biggest story (TSMC Q3 beat + Q4 guide + multi-year capex uplift):**

- **The multi-year "2026-2028 significantly higher than previous three years" framing may be reading forward sell-side pressure rather than demand reality.** The prior-three-years baseline (2023-2025) averaged ~$33B/year in TSMC capex, so even a modest step-up to $65B-$70B average 2026-2028 would clear the "significantly higher" bar. The language is a floor, not a ceiling, and does not of itself quantify the uplift. A skeptic would note that the Q4 guide at $52.3B-$54.3B is strong but exactly aligned with the September monthly-cadence pre-read, so there is no post-call surprise beyond what was already visible to the market from the 9-Oct monthly data. **The counter-case is: Q3 beat was pre-signaled by monthly cadence; Q4 guide is incremental not inflectionary; the multi-year language is a comfortable floor; the AI-demand-"through-2029-2030" framing is a top-line narrative, not a line-item commitment.**
- **The 2027 wafer-price-hike reports (3%-6% per secondary) are also a hedging signal, not pure strength.** A vendor whose demand is as strong as the multi-year framing implies may not need to telegraph a 3%-6% hike publicly; the telegraphing itself hedges against customer pushback on nearer-term node transitions. The N2 ramp cost-drag of 2%-3% on gross margin flagged in prior quarters is real, and the 2027 price hike partially offsets it. A skeptic would read this as "margin defense against ramp costs, dressed as demand-side firming."

**On StepFun Step 5 open weights:** No dominant-story-grade counter-narrative; the vendor-promise bar was cleared on-the-day, the license is published, and the BF16 weights are open. The FP8 manual-approval gate is a standard staged-release approach, not a bait-and-switch. The one credible skeptical angle is the StepFun Model License 1.0 being a new instrument whose terms have not been externally reviewed at length — a pessimistic read is that the license may include terms that materially limit production deployment for Western enterprises; the optimistic read is that the Chinese open-weights frontier is normalizing on vendor-specific licenses rather than adopting Apache 2.0 / MIT, and the trade-off is "legal review cycle" not "unavailable."

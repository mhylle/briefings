Two operative material changes: (1) **anti-distillation**: users may not "use OpenAI models or their outputs to generate, prepare, select or supply training material or signals for models that are intended as alternatives to or substitutes for OpenAI models or services" — a direct bar on OpenAI-output-based distillation into competing frontier or mid-tier models, extending prior "output not to train competitors" language into generation, preparation, selection, and signal-supply categories; (2) **intermediary approval**: services that provide third parties direct access to OpenAI models (model routers, AI gateways, inference-reseller platforms) require prior OpenAI approval. The update also clarifies protections for children and teens and requirements for high-stakes uses (restrictions on automated decisions without human review). The anti-distillation bar materially raises the compliance posture for enterprises running internal fine-tuning or synthetic-data generation pipelines on OpenAI outputs — "intended as alternatives to or substitutes for OpenAI models" is the operative language, and "intent" is the compliance-interpretation hinge. Training on OpenAI outputs for domain-specific derivatives (classification, extraction, formatting) remains permissible if not intended as a substitute; frontier-tier fine-tuning or synthetic data generation for competing-frontier pretraining is explicitly out.
